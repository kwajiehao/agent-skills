# Quill or Reed for the editorial team?

**Recommendation: postpone full adoption and run a comparison pilot.** Quill has an encouraging short-term signal, but the supplied evidence cannot establish that it improves the team's work or beats Reed. This is a simulation using fictional products and sources only.

Quill's vendor study recorded an increase from 5 to 7 accepted items per hour: **40% more output per hour**. That does not mean every editor saves 40% of their work time. Volunteers chose to participate, the vendor funded the study, and participants were not randomly assigned; practice or task differences could help explain the change. No uncertainty range, quality breakdown, or long-term results were supplied. [Vendor study, Source A](sources.md#source-a--quill-vendor-announcement-2026-09-01)

The team's eight-editor pilot is more relevant to its usual work. It recorded 5.0 accepted items per hour without Quill and 5.5 with it: **10% more output per hour**, calculated as `(5.5 − 5.0) / 5.0`. However, each period lasted just one week, task difficulty was not matched, and there was no random assignment. Hours saved and error rates were not recorded, and raw logs are unavailable. The result is an observed change, not proof that Quill caused it. [Internal pilot, Source C](sources.md#source-c--internal-pilot-log-2026-10-01)

Workflow Weekly repeats the vendor figures and adds no independent study. [Sources A](sources.md#source-a--quill-vendor-announcement-2026-09-01), [B](sources.md#source-b--workflow-weekly-2026-09-02), [C](sources.md#source-c--internal-pilot-log-2026-10-01)

For the next pilot, compare Quill, Reed, and the current workflow on similar assignments, with randomized tool order. Track accepted output, errors, editing time, cost, and editor feedback over a longer period. Agree on adoption criteria beforehand. This is a recommendation for resolving the evidence gap, not a finding from the packet.
