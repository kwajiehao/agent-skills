Brief: Explain how spaced repetition works and give an adult language beginner a concise, usable routine. Deliver `report.md` with a simple self-contained diagram. | Spacing helps second-language learning; expanding intervals are not necessary | [Kim & Webb, 2022](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/lang.12479), abstract, paragraphs 1–2 | Synthesis: 48 experiments, 3,411 participants, 98 effect sizes. | Continue recalling words after first success | [Karpicke & Roediger, 2008](https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2008/2008_Karpicke_Roediger_Science.pdf), printed pp. 967–968; PDF pp. 3–4 | College students learned 40 Swahili–English pairs. | Timing matters; longer is not always better | [Cepeda et al., 2008](https://labs.biology.ucsd.edu/rifkin/courses/bieb100/f14/Cepeda_et_al_2008_Psychological_Science_Spacing_Effects_in_Learning_A_Temporal_Ridgeline_of_Optimal_Retention.pdf), printed pp. 1096–1099; PDF pp. 3–6 | 1,354 adults completed three sessions; mean age 34, range 18–72. Adult applicability; factual materials and a two-learning-session design do not prescribe an ongoing language-card algorithm. | Supported |
| Daily ten minutes, five items, days 1/3/7/14; shorter gap after forgetting | Author application of above sources | Manageable starter routine; successful cards spaced, failures relearned. | Explicit suggestions, not trial-derived optimum. Schedule dates are days from initial learning, not successive interval lengths. | Recommendation |
| Practise listening/speaking beyond cards | Author recommendation and boundary of measured outcomes | Studies above primarily establish retention or language-task performance, not everyday conversational fluency. | Add practice matching the desired skill; do not present this routine as proof of fluency gains. | Recommendation / evidence gap |
